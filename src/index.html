Black Friday: your rights when you buy (UK)

Unofficial, and not legal advice. This site is not run by, or connected to, any government body, shop or card provider. It sets out the general rules in the UK, each linked to GOV.UK or the law itself, with the date we checked it. For your own case, use the advice lines listed below.

The short answer

Things you buy in a sale come with the same rights as things you buy at full price. If you bought goods online, by phone or by post, you can usually cancel within 14 days of receiving them, without giving a reason. If goods are faulty or not as described, you can usually reject them for a full refund within 30 days. These rights apply when you buy from a business, not from a private individual.

Sales

Same rights in a saleGOV.UK: customers "have exactly the same rights to refunds when they buy items in a sale as when they buy them at full price." A sign saying a shop does not accept returns cannot take those rights away.GOV.UK, Accepting returns and giving refunds: the law, checked 21 November 2026
Faults you were told aboutA seller does not have to refund you for a fault you knew about when you bought the item.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
Buying from a businessThe Consumer Rights Act protects a "consumer" buying from a "trader", that is, someone acting for their trade or business. Buying from a private individual is different.Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 2, checked 21 November 2026
PresentsA seller only has to accept returns from the person who bought the item. Some sellers accept returns from the person who received a present, for example with a gift receipt, but that is their own policy.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026

Bought online, by phone or by post

Cancel within 14 daysYou can cancel within 14 days of receiving the goods, even if nothing is wrong with them, and you do not have to give a reason. The period ends 14 days after the day the goods reach you (or someone you named, other than the courier). You can also cancel before they arrive.GOV.UK and Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regulation 29(1) and (2) and regulation 30, checked 21 November 2026
Goods, not servicesThis page covers goods. For services, and for digital content you download, the 14 days end 14 days after the day you agree the contract, and other rules apply.Regulation 30(2), checked 21 November 2026
Several deliveriesIf one order arrives on different days, the 14 days end 14 days after the day the last item reaches you.Regulation 30(4), checked 21 November 2026
Sending it backOnce you have told the seller you are cancelling, you have another 14 days to return the item.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
Return postageYou pay the direct cost of sending goods back, unless the seller agreed to pay it or did not tell you before you bought that you would have to.Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013, regulation 35(5), checked 21 November 2026
Your refundThe seller must refund you within 14 days of receiving the item back. They cannot deduct fees unless the item has been used or damaged. They must refund standard delivery; if you paid for a dearer delivery option, they only have to refund the cost of standard delivery.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
Trying it outYou may handle the item as you would in a shop, for example try it on or take it out of the box.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
Not coveredThe 14-day right does not cover, for example: personalised or custom-made items; perishable items such as frozen food or flowers; newspapers and magazines; CDs, DVDs and software once unwrapped; and sealed items that cannot be returned for health or hygiene reasons once opened. For these, the seller only has to refund if they are faulty.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026

Bought in a shop

Changed your mindA shop does not have to refund you if you simply no longer want an item (for example, wrong size or colour), unless you bought it without seeing it. Many shops offer more under their own returns policy; that is their choice.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026

If it is faulty

30 days to rejectIf goods are faulty, not as described, or do not do what they are supposed to, you can reject them for a full refund. This "short-term right to reject" lasts until the end of 30 days, counted from the day after you own the goods, they are delivered and (if needed) installed. For goods expected to perish sooner, it is shorter. After that, you can ask for a repair or replacement. If that does not fix it, you can then reject the item for a refund or ask for a price reduction.GOV.UK and Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 22, section 23 and section 24, checked 21 November 2026
Repair or replaceIf a fault shows up later, the seller must repair or replace the item if you return it within 6 months, unless they can prove it was not faulty when you bought it. After 6 months, they can ask you to prove it was. If a repair or replacement does not fix the problem, you can still reject the item.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
Time to claimYou have up to 6 years to bring a claim (5 years in Scotland). This is a time limit for claims, not a promise that goods will last that long.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
WarrantiesYou have the same right to a free repair or replacement whether or not you have a warranty or guarantee, and even if it has run out.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026
Proof of purchaseA seller may ask for proof that you bought from them, such as a receipt, a bank statement or the packaging.GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026

Paying by credit card

Section 75If you pay by credit card and the seller breaks the contract or misrepresents what you bought, you can have the same claim against the card provider as against the seller. It applies to a single item with a cash price over £100 and up to £30,000. The limit is for each item, not the total of your order.Consumer Credit Act 1974, section 75 and Citizens Advice, Getting your money back if you paid by card or PayPal, checked 21 November 2026
Debit and charge cardsSection 75 does not usually help if you paid by debit card or charge card, or did not buy directly from the seller (for example through a marketplace). Citizens Advice says you can ask your card provider for a "chargeback" instead, though the provider may not agree.Citizens Advice, Getting your money back if you paid by card or PayPal, checked 21 November 2026

Frequently asked questions

Can I return something I bought in a Black Friday sale?

On the same terms as at full price. Goods bought online, by phone or by post: usually within 14 days of receiving them, for any reason (GOV.UK). Faulty or not as described: usually a full refund within 30 days (Consumer Rights Act 2015, section 22). Bought in a shop and not faulty: only if the shop's own policy allows (GOV.UK). Checked 21 November 2026.

When do the 14 days end?

14 days after the day the goods reach you; for one order delivered on different days, 14 days after the day the last item arrives. You can also cancel before they arrive. (Regulations 29 and 30, checked 21 November 2026.)

Do I get the delivery charge back?

When you cancel an online order, the seller must refund standard delivery. If you chose a dearer option, they only have to refund what standard delivery would have cost. Sending the item back is usually at your cost. (GOV.UK and regulation 35, checked 21 November 2026.)

Can a shop refuse a return because I changed my mind?

For something bought in person and not faulty, yes, unless their own policy says otherwise or you bought it without seeing it. Online, phone and post orders are different: you usually have 14 days to cancel. (GOV.UK, checked 21 November 2026.)

Does paying by credit card protect me?

Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974 can make the card provider liable with the seller, for a single item with a cash price over £100 and up to £30,000; the limit is per item, not per order. It does not usually cover debit or charge cards. (legislation.gov.uk and Citizens Advice, Getting your money back if you paid by card or PayPal, checked 21 November 2026.)
